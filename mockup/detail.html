Overview

How these tools help with billing

Billing slips because it happens in the gaps between matter work. You finish a call or an email, and recording the charge gets pushed to “later” — then later never comes.

These tools close that gap. They watch the work you're already doing — your emails, documents and calendar — and draft a billable entry for you. You review it, adjust it if needed, and approve it. Nothing is billed without you.

You can also ask in plain English, like “what have I billed Smith & Co this month?” or “which matters haven't been invoiced since April?”, and get a summary instead of opening the ledger.

Your review stays in charge. These tools draft the entry, but a person approves every line before a client is billed.

What it can do

  • Draft bill entries from your emails, calendar and documents.
  • Answer plain-English questions about what's billed and what's owing.
  • Flag matters with unbilled work before month-end.
How to use

See it in action

The tool runs quietly in the background of your day. At the end of it, it hands you a list of suggested entries — you tick, edit or discard, and the approved ones move to your invoice.

Tools for this job

Three tools, three ways to cover it

These work well together: one captures the time, one turns it into invoices, one keeps the ledger honest.

AskLaw does not endorse any specific tool. Each is listed because it can do the job described — compare them and choose what fits your practice.
PointOne View
  • Captures time automatically from email, calendar and documents.
  • Drafts bill entries you approve before anything is charged.
  • Answers plain-English questions about your matters.
Biller View
  • Turns approved entries into invoices ready to send.
  • Tracks what each client owes and what's overdue.
  • Sends polite reminders so you don't have to chase.
Ledgerline View
  • Reconciles billing against your office account.
  • Flags gaps between work done and work billed.
